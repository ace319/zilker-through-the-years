The story begins in 2002

One weekend became
an Austin tradition.

ACL Festival began at Zilker Park in 2002. It grew from a single weekend into two weekends of music, food, art, and the familiar ritual of meeting beneath the flags.

How ACL Festival came to be

From a television
legend to Zilker Park.

The festival borrowed its name and musical spirit from the long-running PBS concert series Austin City Limits. In 2002, Austin promoters Charles Attal and Charlie Jones imagined an outdoor fall festival that could carry that identity beyond the television studio.

01 · The idea

Built quickly, with Austin at its center

Attal and Jones assembled the inaugural festival in only a few months. The first ACL Fest arrived at Zilker Park on September 28–29, 2002: two days, six stages, and a bill rooted in Americana, country, blues, rock, gospel, and the music of Texas.

02 · Who made it happen

A parks partnership from the beginning

Austin Parks Foundation is the festival's owner and presenter. C3 Presents has produced it on the foundation's behalf since 2002, managing talent, production, operations, and the enormous temporary city that rises in Zilker each fall. Attal and Jones later co-founded C3 with Charlie Walker.

03 · Growth

One weekend became two

The event expanded to three days in 2003 and remained a single-weekend festival through 2012. After Austin City Council approved the change, 2013 became the first year staged across two consecutive weekends. That second Sunday was canceled after exceptionally heavy rain flooded the park.

04 · The pause

2020 left Zilker silent

The COVID-19 pandemic canceled the in-person festival in 2020—the only missing lineup in this archive. ACL returned in 2021, restoring the two-weekend gathering while reflecting the public-health realities of the time.

05 · More than a festival

Music that helps fund parks

A portion of ticket revenue supports Austin Parks Foundation and park improvements across the city. Since 2006, the partnership has provided more than $71 million to Austin's park system, connecting six days of music to year-round public spaces.

06 · ACL today

75,000 people a day

The latest measured attendance, for 2025, was 75,000 people per festival day. Across six days, that equals 450,000 admissions—not necessarily 450,000 different individuals, because many guests attend multiple days. More than 120 artists performed, and the festival generated an estimated $557.8 million for Austin's economy.
